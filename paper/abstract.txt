Portable Document Format (PDF) readers are abundant, but open-source readers that write into a PDF rather than beside it are not: most either render the page and keep annotations in a sidecar, or depend on a large commercial-grade engine. This paper describes PDF Looker Pro, a desktop PDF reader, annotator and page editor whose PDF layer is written from scratch in TypeScript and whose only runtime dependency is the pdf.js rendering engine. We report the engineering rather than a new algorithm, because the interesting failures in this kind of system are invariants that are easy to state and easy to violate. Three are examined in detail: an incremental annotation writer must rebuild a page's /Annots from ``existing minus replaced, plus new'' or it will duplicate every annotation on every save and resurrect every deleted one; a whole-document rewrite must materialise attributes a page previously inherited, or flattening the page tree silently resizes and reorients it; and merging two documents must renumber every reference in the incoming object graph while copying stream bytes verbatim. The annotation model keeps two representations of each mark, a standard subtype for interoperability and a private dictionary for lossless round-tripping, and the paper is explicit about the cost of that duality. The system is verified by 789 behavioural assertions over 31 suites --- 734 against the browser build and 55 against the packaged application --- and we report the defects that methodology caught, including one the authors introduced into the harness itself, together with the case in which it gave a false sense of coverage. The application is 17{,}323 lines of source over 47 files, MIT licensed, and publicly available.
